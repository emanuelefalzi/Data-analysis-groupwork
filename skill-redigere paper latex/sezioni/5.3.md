## DRAFT
[[TAB:T9]] reports OLS estimates for the valuation sample of 7,574 two-round startups. The OLS coefficient θ1 on the gap between the first and the second VC round is 0.109 (p < 0.001). H3 is rejected, opposite sign. Moving from 8 to 19 months across the gap interquartile range goes with a step about 8.9% larger.

The gap × hype OLS coefficient θ3 is −0.016 (p = 0.381), providing no evidence of moderation by hype. In [[FIG:F18]], the predicted log step rises from about 0.9–1.0 at very short gaps to about 1.2 at four years in both cold and hot markets, defined as hype at −1 and +1 standard deviation.

Larger first rounds have an OLS coefficient of −0.139. Older startups and more earlier deals are associated with steps 6.1% and 4.2% smaller, respectively (percentage changes are exp(coefficient) − 1 of the coefficients in [[TAB:T9]]), consistent with part of the growth being priced at round 1. Accelerator-led first rounds go with steps 33% larger. We discuss the pricing interpretation in [[SEC:5.5]] and the post-money and disclosure caveats in [[SEC:5.6]].

For 7,514 startups, [[FIG:F19]] shows a mean total valuation step approximately flat across the first four gap quintiles and higher, at 1.18 log points, in the longest-gap quintile. The median step per year falls from 1.89 to 0.36 log points: startups with short gaps gain value about five times faster per year. We read the positive θ1 as total progress accumulated before round 2 rather than speed of value creation, consistent with the competing prediction in [[SEC:2.4]].
