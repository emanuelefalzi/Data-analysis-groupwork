## DRAFT

[[TAB:T6]] reports logit estimates for exit within five years among one-round and two-round startups of the estimation sample (full outcome window). For the gap between the first and the second VC round, the baseline logit coefficient β1 is −0.089 (p = 0.188; odds ratio 0.915): a 2.7-fold longer gap goes with 8.5% smaller exit odds. The coefficient interval includes zero. H1 is right sign, not significant: we cannot distinguish a small negative association from none.

With hype interactions, the logit coefficients are β1 = −0.071 (p = 0.294) and β3 = −0.056 (p = 0.344). The gap slope is steeper as hype rises, opposite to H2, but the interaction coefficient is not significant. H2 is not supported.

[[TAB:T7]] reports average marginal effects (AMEs) for 4,444 two-round startups. Cold and hot denote hype at −1 and +1 standard deviation. Per unit of ln(1 + months), the AME is about zero in cold markets and −2.1 percentage points in hot markets (p = 0.068); the hot-minus-cold difference is not significant (p = 0.327; [[FIG:F11]]). The linear probability model agrees, with OLS coefficients β1 = −0.0121 (p = 0.271) and β3 = −0.0083 (p = 0.385).

In [[FIG:F12]], predicted exit within five years stays near 22% in cold markets. In hot markets, a gap of about 2 months corresponds to about 26%, compared with about 20% at 4½ years. The bands overlap throughout.

[[FIG:F13]] presents predicted exit probabilities for one-round and two-round startups by hype. The coefficient ρ1 of the two-round group indicator is reported in [[TAB:T6]]; we do not interpret it as an effect, for the reasons discussed in [[SEC:6.2]].

[[FIG:F14]] displays the main exit-model coefficients. Round-1 size has an odds ratio of 1.64, while age at round 1 is associated with 10% higher exit odds. Relative to no identified lead, angel-group, CVC and VC leads are associated with 94%, 55% and 39% higher exit odds, respectively. These associations are larger than the gap association: at average hype, moving across the gap interquartile range corresponds to less than one percentage point in exit probability.
