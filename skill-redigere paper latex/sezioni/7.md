## DRAFT

In the estimation sample of 9,263 US startups with fully observed five-year outcome windows, the gap has a small, negative, statistically insignificant association with exit within five years. The hypothesized moderation by hype is not supported. Longer gaps are associated with less failure and larger total valuation steps.

This pattern is broadly stable in sign across four- to six-year windows, logit, probit and linear probability models, and alternative hype measures. The qualification matters: with past-only hype, β3 remains negative but becomes significant.

For founders, a longer interval before round 2 is not in itself a bad sign. For investors, the gap seems to add little information about exit beyond round-1 characteristics, including round size and lead-investor type. These are conditional associations, not guidance to delay refinancing.

Relevant perspectives in the staging literature provide points of comparison [[CIT-P:cochrane2005, korteweg2010, kaplan2003]]. In our setting, the findings fit the selection account and accumulated-progress reading better than the good-news interpretation ([[SEC:5.5]]). As discussed in [[SEC:6.3]], the gap reflects prior survival and achievement rather than a lever founders can pull. Hype, if anything, makes a longer gap more informative.

Two descriptive extensions remain open: incorporating exit-market conditions at the time of exit to examine the timing account of H2, and comparing growth per year rather than total valuation steps. Designs needed for causal inference are discussed in [[SEC:6.4]].
