## DRAFT

[[TAB:T10]] varies the outcome window between four and six years. Exit β1 and β3 are never significant, while failure β1 is negative and significant in every window and failure β3 stays close to zero. The five-year choice drives none of the results. We report but do not interpret ρ1, the coefficient of the two-round indicator ([[SEC:6.2]]).

[[TAB:T11]] changes the estimator and the hype measure. The probit gives the same signs and significance. Exit at any time gives a large negative gap coefficient that is not comparable: long-gap startups have less follow-up time after round 2, so the estimate mixes the gap with time at risk. With hype computed from past years only, β3 becomes significant but stays negative (−0.050, p = 0.039; 6,593 startups, 27 clusters): the gap association with exit odds is about 5% more negative per standard deviation of past hype. This does not support H2 either; we return to it in [[SEC:5.5]].

In [[TAB:T12]], adding the number of founders, an MBA founder, an AI term and a young lead investor (less than six years old) leaves β1 and β3 essentially unchanged, so omitted team quality of this kind does not explain the null. A young lead goes with lower exit odds (odds ratio 0.716, p = 0.009). [[CIT-N:gompers1996]] finds that young VCs take firms public earlier; in our setting the outcome is exit within five years.

[[FIG:F20]] and [[FIG:F21]] plot the exit and failure coefficients across windows. Further checks leave the picture unchanged. Finer hype measures (sector × quarter, industry group × year and industry group × quarter) give β3 between −0.036 and −0.078, none significant at 5%. Adding the sector's exit share, using heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors (p = 0.358 against 0.344 with clustering; see [[SEC:5.6]]) or removing 367 early one-round exits barely moves β1, β3 or their p-values. VIFs are high only for the group indicator, the gap and their interactions before centring, and at most 2.44 after centring.

Two further checks are in Appendix B. First, [[TAB:BT1]] and [[TAB:BT2]] replace z-scored hype with the raw VC share, the sector's percentage of all VC dollars in the year of round 1 (not centred, mean 27.4%), so β3 is per percentage point and only signs and p-values compare with the main model. β3 is close to zero and not significant for exit and for failure in every window and with no window. The hot − cold difference in the AME of ln(1 + months) on exit, at the mean ± 1 SD of the share, is −0.9 percentage points (p = 0.650), and the share minus its sector mean gives β3 = −0.010 (p = 0.596). H2 is not supported with this measure either.

Second, [[TAB:BT3]] and [[TAB:BT4]] add a squared gap, in ln(1 + months) and in months, with the raw share as hype. Inside the four-, five- and six-year windows no squared term is significant at 5%, and in the five-year models the linear and squared terms are not jointly significant (exit χ²(2) = 0.32, p = 0.851, in logs), although these tests are evaluated at a raw share of zero.

Only with no window does the log gap bend: the squared term is negative and significant for exit and for failure (both p < 0.001), and predicted probabilities of both fall at long gaps ([[FIG:BF1]] and [[FIG:BF2]]). Part of this is time at risk, the same caveat as for exit at any time.

[[FIG:BF3]] shows the two-round indicator and its interaction with ln(1 + months) correlated at 0.94, in line with the uncentred VIFs, while hype correlates at most 0.08 in absolute value with the group indicator, the gap and the round-1 controls.
