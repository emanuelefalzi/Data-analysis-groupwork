## DRAFT

[[CIT-N:li2008]] models the time between financing rounds using a Weibull duration model with right-censoring. In our setting, a survival model starting at round 1, with round 2 as a time-varying covariate, would keep all startups at risk from round 1 and remove the look-ahead in R. It would still estimate associations.

[[CIT-N:howell2020]] finds that VC activity contracts during recessions, particularly at early stages, with capital-supply frictions as a possible mechanism. In our setting, causal identification of the gap would require variation unrelated to venture quality, such as investor-capital shocks delaying rounds. Our data contain no such shock.

[[CIT-N:kerrlerner2014]] uses regression discontinuity around angel-group funding thresholds. In our setting, such designs can isolate financing itself, but our data provide no comparable threshold for the gap between rounds.

Without these designs, our findings remain conditional associations. They describe what an investor observing round 2 can infer from the pace of refinancing, not what a founder gains by raising sooner or later.
