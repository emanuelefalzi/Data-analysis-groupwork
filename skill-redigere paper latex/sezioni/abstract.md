## DRAFT

Does the interval between a startup's first and second venture capital (VC) rounds go with exit, and does sector hype change that association? We analyse PitchBook data on 28,478 US startups with a disclosed first VC round, including 9,263 with fully observed five-year outcome windows. Our two-group design retains one-round and two-round startups, with the gap measured only for the latter; hype is the sector's share of VC dollars in the year of round 1, standardized within sector. Among two-round startups, a longer gap has a negative but statistically insignificant association with exit within five years (right sign, not significant). Moderation by hype is not supported (H2). A longer gap is significantly associated with less failure (H1b: rejected, opposite sign) and a larger total valuation step between rounds (H3: rejected, opposite sign). These are conditional associations among startups that reached round 2, not causal estimates of changing the timing of refinancing.
