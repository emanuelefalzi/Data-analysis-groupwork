## DRAFT

The gap exists only for two-round startups. β1 and β3 therefore describe associations between the gap and outcomes among startups reaching round 2, not all startups with a first VC round.

For two-round startups, the outcome window starts at round 2. Startups with long gaps have already survived longer. Slow raisers are positively selected on survival, pushing the failure coefficient negative.

This negative failure coefficient partly reflects survivorship. It is a real association relevant to an investor observing round 2, not evidence that waiting longer changes failure risk.

Figure 8 in [[SEC:3.3]] shows falling failure, rising still-private shares and flat exit across gap quintiles. This fits survivorship, not a pure distress story.

Given less failure, a larger total valuation step and a flat exit link, we read the gap as a measure of what a startup has already survived and achieved, rather than of its current condition. It describes a state investors observe, not a lever founders can pull.
