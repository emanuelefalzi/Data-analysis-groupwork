## Contenuto (l'unico materiale tuo, insieme ai numeri e alle fonti qui sotto)
1. **The equation**: *logit, exit within five years, R, R × ln gap, hype, interactions, controls, three FE*
   The main model is a logit of exit within five years on both groups, with R, R × ln gap, hype, their interactions, round-1 controls and three sets of fixed effects. It runs first without, then with, hype interactions.
2. **What identifies what**: *β1 within two-round startups, one-round startups, controls, fixed effects, ρ1, ρ2, group comparison*
   Because ln gap is multiplied by R, β1 and β3 come only from variation among two-round startups. One-round startups help estimate the controls and fixed effects. ρ1 and ρ2 compare the two groups.
3. **Interactions in a nonlinear model**: *log-odds, interaction effect on probability, different size and sign, own SE, AMEs*
   In a logit the interaction coefficient is on the log-odds scale. Its effect on the probability can differ in size and sign and needs its own standard error. We also report AMEs of ln gap at hype −1, 0, +1.
4. **Three readings of H2**: *β3, hot-minus-cold AME, delta method, LPM as check*
   H2 is read three ways that should agree: β3 as the change in the log-odds slope, the hot-minus-cold difference in AMEs (delta method), and the linear probability model, where the two coincide.
5. **Samples and clusters**: *9,124 of 9,263, perfect prediction drops, LPM 9,262, 41 clusters, few clusters*
   The logit uses 9,124 startups because Stata drops cells that predict the outcome perfectly. The LPM keeps 9,262. SEs are clustered by sector × year of round 1, giving 41 clusters. With few clusters, p-values can be too small.

## Fonti citabili
### `ai2003`
- **Tesi (vincolante, l'unica cosa che puoi attribuirle)**: in logit and probit the interaction effect is not the interaction coefficient.
- *Contesto dal lettore della routine (NON materiale: nulla che stia solo qui va nel testo)*: In nonlinear models the interaction effect is the cross derivative (or cross difference) of the expected outcome, not the coefficient on the interaction term or its marginal effect. It can be nonzero when the interaction coefficient is zero, it depends on the covariates, it can have different signs across observations, so the sign and t-test of the interaction coefficient do not give it. The formulas are stated to apply to logit and probit; the worked example is a logit.
- *Limiti rispetto al nostro setting*: Methods paper; derives the correct interaction effect and its standard errors for a single interaction, with no startup or VC data. Our model has two interactions (two_rounds x heat, log_gap_r2 x heat), which the paper covers only as 'analogous'.
- *Verbatim (solo per vedere dove finisce la fonte, MAI da copiare)*: "The key point of this paper is that the interaction effect is found by computing cross derivatives (or differences), not by just looking at the coefficient on the interaction term."

### `mize2019`
- **Tesi (vincolante, l'unica cosa che puoi attribuirle)**: best practice is to estimate and show nonlinear interactions with marginal effects.
- *Contesto dal lettore della routine (NON materiale: nulla che stia solo qui va nel testo)*: Mize advises that, for nonlinear models such as logit, the size and significance of interaction effects be judged with marginal effects and tests of second differences, plotting predictions, and ignoring the product-term coefficient. Absent specific values of interest, average marginal effects summarise whether an interaction is present on average. This matches the tesi that nonlinear interactions are best estimated and shown with marginal effects.
- *Limiti rispetto al nostro setting*: Sociology-oriented guidance with survey examples; not specific to startup/VC data or to exit outcomes. It is a methods recommendation, not a finding about our data.
- *Verbatim (solo per vedere dove finisce la fonte, MAI da copiare)*: "Determine the size and significance of the effects of interest using marginal effects, not regression coefficients."

### `cameron2008`
- **Tesi (vincolante, l'unica cosa che puoi attribuirle)**: with few clusters (5–30) cluster-robust tests over-reject; the wild cluster bootstrap-t brings size near 5% (linear models).
- *Contesto dal lettore della routine (NON materiale: nulla che stia solo qui va nel testo)*: The txt says that Wald tests based on cluster-robust standard errors over-reject when the number of clusters is small (five to thirty), because the standard errors are biased downwards; in simulations a nominal 5% test rejects about 10% or more of the time. The wild cluster bootstrap-t brings rejection rates close to 0.05 in these linear-model simulations, but the txt also notes that bootstrap results depend on the resampling method and on discreteness, and the wild cluster bootstrap-se (not -t) under-rejects for G <= 10. The txt speaks of over-rejection of tests, not of p-values directly, and its evidence is for linear models.
- *Limiti rispetto al nostro setting*: Simulations and applications are linear OLS models (mostly difference-in-differences), not logit; G between 5 and 30, while our main exit models have 41 clusters (sector x year).
- *Verbatim (solo per vedere dove finisce la fonte, MAI da copiare)*: "asymptotic tests can over-reject, however, with few (five to thirty)"

### `abadie2023` — **solo rimando**: la fonte e' gia' presentata altrove; qui la citi con la stessa tesi, in poche parole.
- **Tesi (vincolante, l'unica cosa che puoi attribuirle)**: as in 2.5.
- *Contesto dal lettore della routine (NON materiale: nulla che stia solo qui va nel testo)*: The paper argues that, when sampling is not clustered, standard errors should be clustered at the treatment assignment level; with clustered assignment one clusters at the level of the assignment. Clustering is also warranted by clustered sampling, and when sampling and assignment are both random clustering is not appropriate. The framework concerns treatment assignment, not regressors in general.
- *Limiti rispetto al nostro setting*: The paper is about treatment effects with sampling and assignment processes (experiments, difference-in-differences); our heat and gap regressors are observational, not assigned by a known mechanism, and clusters are sector x year.
- *Verbatim (solo per vedere dove finisce la fonte, MAI da copiare)*: "With clustered assignment, one should cluster the standard errors at the level of the assignment."

**3.3 Descriptive statistics**

In the estimation sample, two-round startups differ sharply from one-round startups ([[TAB:T5]]). Their exit-within-five-years shares are 22.2% and 15.6%, respectively; failure shares are 12.4% and 31.6%. Both differences are significant in t-tests. Two-round startups also raised larger first VC rounds and were younger at round 1.

The gap between the first and the second VC round has a median of about 13 months and a mean of 14.7 months among two-round startups. Its long right tail, shown in [[FIG:F2]], motivates the log specification and the use of the interquartile range for magnitudes.

[[FIG:F3]] shows the cohort truncation: the one-round share jumps after 2019 because recent cohorts had insufficient time to raise another VC round. Restricting the comparison to fully observed outcome windows removes this mechanical pattern from the group comparison.

Hype varies over time within every sector ([[FIG:F4]]). IT is cold in 2015–2019, healthcare peaks in 2020, and Energy has an outlier in 2012, when very few deals were recorded. Within-sector variation identifies H2 once sector fixed effects remove permanent differences between sectors.

[[FIG:F5]] presents exit and failure within five years by sector, while [[FIG:F6]] presents exit within five years by group and market hype. [[FIG:F7]] breaks down exit within five years by gap quintile and market hype. We use these raw patterns descriptively; the H1 and H2 tests are in [[SEC:5.1]] and [[SEC:5.2]].

Across gap quintiles, the exit share barely moves, the failure share falls and the share still private rises ([[FIG:F8]]). A long gap is followed more by staying private than by exit. These are raw shares of competing outcomes, without controls.
