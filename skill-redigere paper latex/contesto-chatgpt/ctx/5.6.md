## Contenuto (l'unico materiale tuo, insieme ai numeri e alle fonti qui sotto)
1. **Few clusters**: *41 clusters, sector × year, p-values too small, wild cluster bootstrap*
   Inference in the main exit models rests on 41 clusters. Robust SEs give almost the same p-values, but a wild cluster bootstrap would be the safer test and is not in our do-file.
2. **Competing outcomes modelled separately**: *exit, failure, still private, multinomial, competing risks*
   Exit and failure are competing outcomes, with a third state, still private, absorbing the rest. Separate logits do not use the three states jointly. A multinomial or competing-risks model would.
3. **Valuation measurement**: *post-money, fair value, terms ignored, disclosure, selected sample*
   The step uses post-money valuations, which overstate fair value and ignore contract terms other than the equity share, and it is observed for about half of two-round startups. If disclosure follows good rounds, the sample is selected towards high steps.
4. **What the sample represents**: *course cohort, hype from cohort's own deals, ageing cohort, disclosed round 1 only*
   Hype is built from deals of the course cohort (founded 2012–2019), so annual shares partly reflect its ageing. Dropping undisclosed round-1 amounts keeps only startups that disclosed. Both limit how far results generalise.

### `cameron2008`
- **Tesi (vincolante, l'unica cosa che puoi attribuirle)**: wild cluster bootstrap for few clusters.
- *Contesto dal lettore della routine (NON materiale: nulla che stia solo qui va nel testo)*: The txt shows, by Monte Carlo and a replication of Bertrand, Duflo and Mullainathan (2004), that the wild cluster bootstrap-t gives rejection rates close to 0.05 with few clusters, performing well with as few as six clusters. The authors recommend it for more accurate cluster-robust inference when there are few clusters. Evidence is for linear models.
- *Limiti rispetto al nostro setting*: Linear OLS models with G = 5 to 30; our models are logit with 41 clusters, so the result is a method reference, not a test on our model.
- *Verbatim (solo per vedere dove finisce la fonte, MAI da copiare)*: "wild cluster bootstrap methods perform well in cases with as"

### `gornall2020`
- **Tesi (vincolante, l'unica cosa che puoi attribuirle)**: post-money overstates fair value for US unicorns (48% on average).
- *Contesto dal lettore della routine (NON materiale: nulla che stia solo qui va nel testo)*: The txt shows post-money valuations overstate fair value in all 135 US unicorns studied, because the post-money formula treats all shares as equal to the latest preferred shares. It does not test this for VC-backed companies in general, so the tesi is wider than the txt.
- *Limiti rispetto al nostro setting*: Only 135 US unicorns (valuations above $1 billion) as of their most recent round, valued with a contingent-claims model on terms from charters; our sample is all US startups with a first VC round in 2012-2019 and mostly small post-money valuations.
- *Verbatim (solo per vedere dove finisce la fonte, MAI da copiare)*: "valuations average 48% above fair value, with 14 being more than 100% above."

### `ewens2022` — **solo rimando**: la fonte e' gia' presentata altrove; qui la citi con la stessa tesi, in poche parole.
- **Tesi (vincolante, l'unica cosa che puoi attribuirle)**: as in 2.4.

### `cochrane2005`

## Sezione precedente (gia' scritta, per la continuita')
**5.5 Discussion of the results**

We read the four verdicts in [[TAB:T13]] together: among two-round startups, a longer gap between the first and the second VC round is associated with less failure, a larger valuation step and, at most, a slightly smaller probability of exit within five years. A long gap is not a distress signal here.

Shorter rounds were associated with higher agency costs or greater investor distance, consistent with costlier monitoring [[CIT-P:gompers1995, tian2011]]. In our setting, we interpret this short leash as consistent with greater risk among short-gap startups. Our argument is that risk fattens both tails, fitting higher failure alongside a weak exit association.

We also argue that long-gap startups have passed a longer test before round 2 and accumulated more progress, priced at that round. The valuation-step elasticity far below one means a larger total step but slower growth per month. Observed valuations form a selected sample because observation occurs at refinancing or exit [[CIT-P:cochrane2005, korteweg2010]]. In our setting, the longer-test reading is consistent with survivorship.

The negative logit interaction coefficient β3 fits the signal-extraction alternative in [[SEC:2.3]]: a long gap is more informative in hot markets. The past-only hype check is reported in [[SEC:5.4]]. We also consider attenuation: hype at round 1 may poorly proxy exit-market conditions years later, which would bias β3 towards zero.

The strongest exit predictors are already visible at round 1: size, age and lead type. [[TAB:T14]] compares our estimates with the literature. Corporate-VC-led first rounds were less likely to reach the next round [[CIT-P:hochberg2007]]. In our setting, the outcome is exit within five years, so we do not claim a match. [[GAP: Specify the proposed links concerning CVC as acquirer, VC networks, angel groups and accelerators.]] Sorting outweighed investor influence in IPO outcomes, while surveyed VCs ranked selection above value-add [[CIT-P:sorensen2007, gompers2020]]. In our setting, we read these coefficients as mixing selection of better startups with what investors add.
