## Contenuto (l'unico materiale tuo, insieme ai numeri e alle fonti qui sotto)
1. **Common causes and selection**: *team, deal selection, sorting, investor influence, quality, round-1 timing*
   VCs select mainly on the team, and sorting of better ventures to better investors explains more of outcomes than investor influence. Anything correlated with quality looks predictive, so we control only for variables fixed at round 1.
2. **Round-1 size**: *runway (our argument), investor conviction, syndicate quality, smaller experimental cheques*
   We argue that a larger first round buys runway and reflects investor conviction and syndicate quality. Cheaper experimentation means small first rounds are often experimental bets. Expected: more exit, less failure, a smaller step.
3. **Age and earlier deals at round 1**: *up-or-out, young firms, track record, angel and seed financing, experimentation already funded*
   Young firms grow fast but exit the market often. Older firms at round 1 have survived and have a track record. Earlier deals capture how much experimentation angels or others already financed. Expected: less failure, a smaller step.
4. **Lead-investor type**: *professionalisation, networks, reputation, monitoring, CVC as acquirer, angel groups, accelerators, grandstanding*
   Investor types select and add differently. VCs professionalise. Networked and reputable VCs see more exits. CVCs back innovative firms and buy them. Angel-funded ventures are more likely to survive. Some accelerators speed up development. Coefficients mix selection and influence.
5. **Fixed effects and clustering**: *HQ state, sector, year-quarter, geography, industry exit rates, exit windows, sector × year clusters*
   State FE absorb the geographic concentration of VC. Sector FE absorb industry differences in success rates and in how fast firms mature. Year-quarter FE absorb exit-market windows. SEs are clustered by sector × year of round 1, the level at which hype is assigned.

## Sezione precedente (gia' scritta, per la continuita')
**2.4 Valuation steps**

Refinancing is more likely after value has risen. Observed returns between rounds are selected upward [[CIT-P:cochrane2005, korteweg2010]]. In our setting, if fast rounds follow good news, they should carry larger valuation steps. H3 predicts that, among two-round startups, a longer gap is associated with a smaller valuation step (θ1 < 0), defined as ln(post-money valuation at round 2 / post-money valuation at round 1).

We argue that rounds are priced on accumulated venture achievements. A longer gap allows more time for progress, potentially going with a larger total valuation step even with slower monthly value growth (θ1 > 0). Milestones govern disbursement of committed capital and some contractual rights within a round [[CIT-P:kaplan2003]]. In our setting, the accumulated-progress counter-prediction is our argument, not a literature prediction about the timing of the next round.

Later investors hold senior preferred shares, so post-money valuations overstate fair value. Reported post-money valuations averaged 48% above fair value for 135 US unicorns [[CIT-P:gornall2020]]. Post-money valuation poorly measures firm value because contractual provisions beyond equity ownership are omitted [[CIT-P:ewens2022]]. In a study of MIT start-up offers, entrepreneurs accepted lower valuations from more reputable VCs [[CIT-P:hsu2004]]. In our setting, measured valuation steps therefore mix value with contractual terms and bargaining power. Measurement limitations are discussed in [[SEC:5.6]].

Prices were positively associated with capital inflows [[CIT-P:gompers2000]]. In our setting, hype could be associated with the valuation step directly and could moderate its association with the gap. We therefore include hype and gap × hype in the H3 model, with θ3 capturing the latter association.
