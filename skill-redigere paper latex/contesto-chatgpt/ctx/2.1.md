## Contenuto (l'unico materiale tuo, insieme ai numeri e alle fonti qui sotto)
1. **Staging as a real option**: *abandonment option, milestones, contingent rights, screening, cut-off of poor ventures*
   VC is staged so investors can abandon after new information. Contracts tie rights to milestones, and staging screens out ventures with poor early results. On this reading the gap is the time a venture needs to produce investable news.
2. **Learning and experimentation**: *Bayesian learning, belief decline, positive signals, experimentation, faster refinancing*
   In learning models each period without good news lowers the investor's belief, so good projects are refinanced sooner. Experimentation accounts agree: early rounds buy information, and fast positive signals bring fast refinancing. A short gap reads as good news.
3. **Staging as a leash**: *agency costs, monitoring, distance, shorter rounds, our step to risk*
   A second strand treats round length as the investor's governance choice. Rounds get shorter when agency or monitoring costs are higher. We argue that a short gap may then mark a venture that is hard to monitor and riskier, not a good one: the competing prediction (I-04).
4. **Evidence and the gap we fill**: *number and size of rounds, patents' signalling, information priced by round 2, untested link*
   Evidence links outcomes to the number and size of rounds, rarely to time. Patent signals fade by round 2 because investors already know more. No study we know relates gap to exit while keeping firms that never raised again.
5. **H1 stated**: *β1 < 0, longer gap, lower exit probability, two-round startups, competing β1 ≥ 0*
   H1: among startups that raise a second round, a longer gap is associated with a lower probability of exit within five years. The leash view predicts zero or positive. β1 is the coefficient of R × ln gap.

## Fonti citabili
### `gompers1995`
- **Tesi (vincolante, l'unica cosa che puoi attribuirle)**: staging as monitoring; duration falls with intangibility, R&D and market-to-book.
- *Contesto dal lettore della routine (NON materiale: nulla che stia solo qui va nel testo)*: Gompers argues that staged financing lets the VC gather information, monitor and keep the option to abandon the project. In Weibull regressions, duration falls as the industry tangible-assets ratio falls, as market-to-book rises and as R&D intensity rises, each significant between the 7% and 1% levels. When all three are included together, tangibility stays most significant, market-to-book drops out completely and R&D is weaker.
- *Limiti rispetto al nostro setting*: US-only 1961-1992 (Venture Economics), not 2012-2019; duration measured with industry-level proxies, not firm risk; outcomes include IPO, merger, bankruptcy; no exit-within-five-years window.
- *Verbatim (solo per vedere dove finisce la fonte, MAI da copiare)*: "In Panel A, financing duration declines with decreases in the industry ratio of tangible assets to total assets, increases in the market-to-book ratio, and greater R&D intensity."

### `neher1999`
- **Tesi (vincolante, l'unica cosa che puoi attribuirle)**: staging solves an agency problem between founder and investor.
- *Contesto dal lettore della routine (NON materiale: nulla che stia solo qui va nel testo)*: The paper models staging as the answer to an agency problem between entrepreneur and venture capitalist, framed as a commitment/hold-up problem: the entrepreneur cannot commit not to renegotiate the investor's claim once the money is sunk. Staging keeps each round small and lets early rounds build collateral, so later rounds are protected. It stresses that this is a symmetric-information model, different from Gompers' asymmetric-information and monitoring view, and from real-option views.
- *Limiti rispetto al nostro setting*: Pure theory with perfect certainty and symmetric information; no data, no exit outcomes and no timing of the second round. It explains why financing is staged, not how long the gap should be.
- *Verbatim (solo per vedere dove finisce la fonte, MAI da copiare)*: "rather than the outcome of the agency problem that exists between the entrepreneur and the venture capitalist"

### `cornelli2003`
- **Tesi (vincolante, l'unica cosa che puoi attribuirle)**: staging gives the VC the option to abandon; convertibles deter window dressing of interim results.
- *Contesto dal lettore della routine (NON materiale: nulla che stia solo qui va nel testo)*: The paper states that stage financing gives the venture capitalist the option to abandon the venture when the project's forward-looking value is negative. Its model shows that this option induces the entrepreneur to engage in window dressing, biasing the interim signal to reduce the probability of liquidation. Convertible securities are shown to deter this behaviour, because window dressing also raises the probability that the VC converts. The contribution is that convertibles dominate debt-plus-equity within staging, not that convertibles are what keeps the option to stop.
- *Limiti rispetto al nostro setting*: Pure theory with no data; it concerns contract design (convertible debt or warrants) and not the timing of rounds, the gap between rounds or exit outcomes.
- *Verbatim (solo per vedere dove finisce la fonte, MAI da copiare)*: "The venture capitalist retains the option to abandon the venture whenever the forward looking net present value of the project is negative."

### `kaplan2003`
- **Tesi (vincolante, l'unica cosa che puoi attribuirle)**: VC contracts make cash-flow and control rights depend on observable milestones.
- *Contesto dal lettore della routine (NON materiale: nulla che stia solo qui va nel testo)*: The paper finds that cash flow rights and control rights in VC contracts can be separated and made contingent on observable and verifiable measures of performance. It also reports that VC-controlled ventures are more likely to make the entrepreneur's equity claim and the release of committed funds contingent on performance milestones. The contingency is on performance measures and milestones, in a sample of 213 rounds.
- *Limiti rispetto al nostro setting*: Contracts of 14 VC firms, rounds 1986-1999, not our PitchBook 2012-2019 sample; contract terms are not observed in our data.
- *Verbatim (solo per vedere dove finisce la fonte, MAI da copiare)*: "cash flow rights and control rights can be separated and made contingent on observable and verifiable measures of performance"

### `dahiya2012`
- **Tesi (vincolante, l'unica cosa che puoi attribuirle)**: staging as a screen that cuts ventures with poor early results.
- *Contesto dal lettore della routine (NON materiale: nulla che stia solo qui va nel testo)*: In the model, the interim evaluation gives the option to abandon the project if early returns are low; the VC continues only if the expected value of continuing exceeds the outside options. The abstract says staging lets investors abandon ventures with low early returns and so sorts good projects from bad.
- *Limiti rispetto al nostro setting*: Theory model (two stages, risk-neutral VC) tested on VentureXpert US financing rounds on the ratio of early to late investment; it does not test abandonment rates or exits, and is not a 2012-2019 PitchBook exit_5y design.
- *Verbatim (solo per vedere dove finisce la fonte, MAI da copiare)*: "The main reason to conduct an evaluation halfway through a project is that it provides the option to abandon the project if the early returns are low."

### `bergemann2005`
- **Tesi (vincolante, l'unica cosa che puoi attribuirle)**: each period without success lowers the investor's belief that the project is good (theory).
- *Contesto dal lettore della routine (NON materiale: nulla che stia solo qui va nel testo)*: In the model the posterior belief that the project is good falls in every period in which no success arrives (Bayes' rule). The model has only success or no success, not 'good news' as a graded signal. The decline is slower when beliefs are already near 0 or 1.
