## Convenzioni
- First person plural (we) is allowed for the design and our reading of the results; never first person singular.
- Present tense for theory and for what sources argue; past tense for the design and results of single studies; present tense for our own results as they appear in the tables.
- Associations only: never effect, impact, causes, leads to, reduces, increases for the gap or for hype. Write is associated with, goes with.
- Every number says what it is (logit coefficient, odds ratio, AME in percentage points, OLS coefficient), its p-value and its sample.
- Hypothesis verdicts use the exact wording of the paper (right sign, not significant; rejected, opposite sign; not supported).
- No verbatim quotes from sources: attributed paraphrase only.
- No bullet lists in the body: continuous prose. Tables and figures carry the numbers; the text points to them and does not repeat every cell.

## Verdicts and causal language (RULES.md)

- Hypothesis verdicts use the paper's Table 13 wording only: **right sign, not significant** · **rejected, opposite sign** · **not supported**. Never "no effect" or "confirmed".
- About the gap or hype, never write *effect, impact, causes, leads to, reduces, increases, drives*. Write **is associated with**, **goes with**.
- "Stable in sign" across hype measures, not "stable": with past-only hype β3 is significant (log L2605).


## Contenuto (l'unico materiale tuo, insieme ai numeri e alle fonti qui sotto)
1. **Survival model from round 1**: *hazard model, round 2 as time-varying covariate, all at risk from round 1, removes look-ahead*
   A survival model with round 2 as a time-varying covariate would treat all startups as at risk from round 1 and remove the look-ahead in R. It still estimates associations but fixes 6.2.
2. **Exogenous variation in the gap**: *supply shock, investor capital, natural experiment, quality-unrelated delay*
   A causal gap effect needs variation in the gap unrelated to venture quality, such as shocks to investors' capital that delay rounds for reasons outside the venture. We have no such shock in our data.
3. **Discontinuity designs**: *regression discontinuity, funding threshold, comparable startups, financing effect*
   Discontinuity designs compare startups just above and just below a funding threshold, which isolates the effect of financing itself. Nothing in our data provides such a threshold for the gap between rounds, so this route is closed here.
4. **What we can honestly claim**: *conditional associations, investor at round 2, founder choice, descriptive claim*
   Without these designs the paper offers conditional associations. They describe what an investor at round 2 can infer from the pace of refinancing, not what a founder gains by raising faster or slower.

## Fonti citabili
### `kerrlerner2014`
- **Tesi (vincolante, l'unica cosa che puoi attribuirle)**: regression-discontinuity evidence on angel-group funding (a model of point 3).
- *Contesto dal lettore della routine (NON materiale: nulla che stia solo qui va nel testo)*: The paper's third set of findings uses a regression discontinuity design on ventures just above and below the funding threshold of two US angel groups, to remove the endogeneity of funding. It finds more survival and better operations above the threshold, qualitative but non-significant support for exit, and no difference in follow-on financing.
- *Limiti rispetto al nostro setting*: Two large, professionally managed US angel groups (Tech Coast Angels, CommonAngels), 130 deals pitched 2001-2006, outcomes to Dec 2010; angels, not VC; the authors say effects are likely at the upper end of the angel population. Not a VC setting and not a gap or hype design.
- *Verbatim (solo per vedere dove finisce la fonte, MAI da copiare)*: "Our third set of findings considers ventures just above and below the funding threshold by using the regression discontinuity methodology"

### `li2008`
- **Tesi (vincolante, l'unica cosa che puoi attribuirle)**: Weibull duration model of time between rounds, with right-censoring (a model of point 1).
- *Contesto dal lettore della routine (NON materiale: nulla che stia solo qui va nel testo)*: Same point as the other methods row: Li studies the time between adjacent financing rounds with a Weibull accelerated-failure-time model on the log of duration, accounting for right-censoring. A semi-parametric Cox model and exponential or log-normal models give unaltered results, but they are not the main specification.
- *Limiti rispetto al nostro setting*: Round duration is the outcome in Li; in our design the gap is an explanatory variable for exit.
- *Verbatim (solo per vedere dove finisce la fonte, MAI da copiare)*: "The empirical analysis focuses on the funding duration, i.e., the time elapsed between two adjacent rounds of financing for a portfolio company."

### `howell2020`
- **Tesi (vincolante, l'unica cosa che puoi attribuirle)**: VC activity falls in recessions, mostly early-stage; capital-supply frictions are one likely mechanism.
- *Contesto dal lettore della routine (NON materiale: nulla che stia solo qui va nel testo)*: The paper shows that VC deal activity is strongly pro-cyclical: it falls in recessions, especially early-stage. On whether the cause is supply, it is more cautious: all forces (investment opportunities, entrepreneurs' human capital, supply frictions) are likely at play, and the early-stage concentration and the fundraising-timing test are said to suggest or support a capital-supply friction. In the COVID-19 episode the authors argue supply was not constrained.
- *Limiti rispetto al nostro setting*: Recession and COVID-19 shocks, not within-sector hype variation in a boom period; patent-quality tests, no startup exit outcomes.
- *Verbatim (solo per vedere dove finisce la fonte, MAI da copiare)*: "All of these forces are likely at play, to a greater or lesser extent, in recessions."
