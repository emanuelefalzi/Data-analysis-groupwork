## Contenuto (l'unico materiale tuo, insieme ai numeri e alle fonti qui sotto)
1. **Abandonment predicts more failure**: *option to abandon, stalled ventures, cut off, long gap, failure*
   If refinancing follows good news, investors cut off stalled ventures. A long gap should then go with more failure. This follows directly from the real-option and screening models of 2.1, applied to the bad tail.
2. **Investors do not always abandon**: *escalation, institutional pressure, tolerance for failure, innovation contracts*
   Investors keep funding under-performing ventures for political and institutional reasons. Contracts that tolerate early failure can be optimal for innovation. Both weaken the link between a long gap and abandonment, and so between a long gap and failure.
3. **Survivorship at refinancing**: *observed only at financing events, selection, value increase, positive selection on survival*
   Private-firm values and outcomes are seen only at financing events, and firms are refinanced only if alive and worth more. Returns at refinancing are therefore selected upward. This predicts that conditioning on refinancing makes slow raisers look safer.
4. **Life-cycle timing of failure**: *VC-backed vs non-VC firms, timing of failure, timing of acquisition, clock*
   VC-backed firms differ from comparable firms in when they fail and when they are acquired. When the clock starts therefore matters for how failure is measured, which motivates our explicit outcome window.
5. **H1b stated**: *H1b, failure within five years, gap coefficient > 0, competing negative sign, survivorship*
   H1b: a longer gap is associated with a higher probability of failure within five years among two-round startups. The competing prediction, from survivorship and tolerance for failure, is a negative coefficient on the gap in the failure model.

- **Tesi (vincolante, l'unica cosa che puoi attribuirle)**: abandonment of ventures whose early returns fall short (theory).
- *Contesto dal lettore della routine (NON materiale: nulla che stia solo qui va nel testo)*: The txt speaks of abandoning or terminating projects after the first stage when early returns fall below a milestone; it also proposes the share of firms abandoned at the interim stage as an empirical proxy for the milestone. ### `guler2007`
- **Tesi (vincolante, l'unica cosa che puoi attribuirle)**: VCs become less likely to terminate as rounds accumulate, despite falling expected returns; politics may contribute.
- *Contesto dal lettore della routine (NON materiale: nulla che stia solo qui va nel testo)*: The txt finds that VC firms become less likely to terminate their investment in a venture as the number of financing rounds rises, even though the likelihood and size of successful outcomes decline with rounds. It argues that intraorganizational politics, co-investor pressures and industry norms may contribute to this reluctance to terminate. The wording is hedged (may, in part, the results suggest); the tesi turns it into a flat statement about funding under-performing ventures and about reasons.
- *Limiti rispetto al nostro setting*: Ventures founded 1989-1993 (VentureXpert), not 2012-2019 PitchBook; outcome is the VC firm's termination of its own investment, not startup exit within five years.
- *Verbatim (solo per vedere dove finisce la fonte, MAI da copiare)*: "The political and institutional features of the VC investment process may create perverse incentives to continue investment, even as the likelihood of success declines."

### `manso2011`
- **Tesi (vincolante, l'unica cosa che puoi attribuirle)**: contracts that tolerate early failure are optimal to motivate innovation.
- *Contesto dal lettore della routine (NON materiale: nulla che stia solo qui va nel testo)*: In a principal-agent model with a bandit problem, the optimal contract that motivates exploration (innovation) exhibits substantial tolerance, or even reward, for early failure, together with reward for long-term success. Standard pay-for-performance schemes that punish early failure may hurt innovation. The result is derived in the model (Proposition 2), not tested on data.
- *Limiti rispetto al nostro setting*: Theory of managerial or employee incentive contracts, not VC financing, startups or exit; no data. It supports the logic that early failure is tolerated, not a result about funding gaps.
- *Verbatim (solo per vedere dove finisce la fonte, MAI da copiare)*: "the optimal incentive scheme that motivates innovation exhibits substantial tolerance (or even reward) for early failure and reward for long-term success"

- *Contesto dal lettore della routine (NON materiale: nulla che stia solo qui va nel testo)*: Cochrane states that valuations are observed only when a firm goes public, receives new financing or is acquired, and that overcoming the resulting selection bias is the central hurdle in evaluating VC.

- *Verbatim (solo per vedere dove finisce la fonte, MAI da copiare)*: "We observe valuations only when a ﬁrm goes public, receives new ﬁnancing, or is acquired."

### `korteweg2010`
- **Tesi (vincolante, l'unica cosa che puoi attribuirle)**: corrects VC risk and return for the selection of observed valuations.
- *Contesto dal lettore della routine (NON materiale: nulla che stia solo qui va nel testo)*: The paper develops a dynamic sample-selection model and estimates it on VC data to correct risk and return for selection of observed valuations. ### `puri2012`
- **Tesi (vincolante, l'unica cosa che puoi attribuirle)**: VC-backed and non-VC firms differ in the timing of failure and acquisition.
- *Contesto dal lettore della routine (NON materiale: nulla che stia solo qui va nel testo)*: The paper compares VC-financed and matched non-VC-financed US firms and finds that timing differs for failure: the lower cumulative failure rate of VC firms comes from much lower failure in the first few years after VC, then the gap tapers off. - *Verbatim (solo per vedere dove finisce la fonte, MAI da copiare)*: "we see that the probability that a VC-financed firm is acquired increases each year after the firm first receives VC-financing relative to non-VC-financed firms"

## Sezione precedente (gia' scritta, per la continuita')
**2.1 What the time between rounds measures**

Staging gives investors an abandonment option [[CIT-P:cornelli2003]]. VC-controlled ventures are more likely to have cash-flow and control rights contingent on observable milestones [[CIT-P:kaplan2003]]. Staging models predict screening through discontinuing ventures with poor early results [[CIT-P:dahiya2012]]. In our setting, investors can abandon after new information. On this reading, the gap between the first and the second VC round represents the time needed to produce investable news.

Under Bayesian learning, each period without success lowers investors' belief that a project is good [[CIT-P:bergemann2005]]. In software and services, the VC round is an experiment that buys information [[CIT-P:kerrnanda2014, ewens2018]]. In our setting, fast positive signals go with faster refinancing of good projects: a short gap reads as good news.

The leash view treats round length as a governance choice: rounds are shorter when agency or monitoring costs are higher. Greater investor distance was associated with more rounds and shorter intervals between them [[CIT-P:tian2011]]. In our setting, we argue that a short gap may mark a venture that is harder to monitor and riskier, giving the competing prediction.

Evidence links outcomes to the number and size of rounds, rarely to time. Patent applications were associated with larger first-round funding but not second-round funding in US biotechnology [[CIT-P:hoenen2014]]. We read the fading patent signal as reflecting information already known to investors by round 2. We know of no study relating the gap to exit while retaining one-round startups.

H1 states that, among two-round startups, a longer gap is associated with a smaller probability of exit within five years. For β1, the coefficient of R × ln(1 + months), this predicts β1 < 0. The competing leash view predicts β1 ≥ 0.
