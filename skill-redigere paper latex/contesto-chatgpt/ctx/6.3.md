## Contenuto (l'unico materiale tuo, insieme ai numeri e alle fonti qui sotto)
1. **β1 and β3 describe refinanced startups only**: *conditional on round 2, refinanced startups, scope, not all round-1 startups*
   The gap exists only for startups that reached round 2, so β1 and β3 describe how speed relates to outcomes among refinanced startups, not among all startups that raised a first round.
2. **The window starts after the gap**: *outcome window at round 2, survived the gap, positive selection, slow raisers survivors*
   For two-round startups the window opens at round 2, so a slow raiser has already survived a long gap. Slow raisers are positively selected on survival, which pushes the failure coefficient negative.
3. **What survivorship means for the failure result**: *real association, investor at round 2, not the effect of waiting, failure result*
   The negative failure coefficient is partly this survivorship. It is a real association, and it is exactly what an investor observing round 2 sees, but it is not the effect of waiting longer before raising again.
4. **Raw evidence that fits**: *still private rises, failure falls, exit flat, Figure 8*
   The raw three-way split in Figure 8 fits this reading: across gap quintiles, slow raisers fail less and stay private more, without exiting more. Survivorship predicts exactly this pattern; a pure distress story does not.
5. **What the gap measures (I-02)**: *survived and achieved, not how it is doing, state not lever, founder vs investor*
   We read the gap as a measure of how much a startup has already survived and achieved before round 2, not of how it is doing: less failure, a larger total step and a flat exit link. It describes a state an investor observes, not a lever a founder can pull.

## Fonti citabili
### `cochrane2005` (citata insieme a korteweg2010)
- **Tesi (vincolante, l'unica cosa che puoi attribuirle)**: valuations observed only at refinancing or exit are a selected sample.
- *Contesto dal lettore della routine (NON materiale: nulla che stia solo qui va nel testo)*: Returns are observed only at new financing, IPO or acquisition, and the probability of these rises with value, so observed outcomes are selected. The txt concerns returns/valuations, not exit or failure outcomes in general.
- *Limiti rispetto al nostro setting*: Individual VC projects, VentureOne data to 2000 (dot-com era); returns, not exit probabilities; selection concerns valuations, not our exit outcome.
- *Verbatim (solo per vedere dove finisce la fonte, MAI da copiare)*: "projects are more likely to get new ﬁnancing, and especially to go public, when their value has risen."

### `korteweg2010` (citata insieme a cochrane2005)
- **Tesi (vincolante, l'unica cosa che puoi attribuirle)**: valuations observed only at refinancing or exit are a selected sample.
- *Contesto dal lettore della routine (NON materiale: nulla che stia solo qui va nel testo)*: The paper supports that valuations are observed only at refinancing or exit events and that this selects the sample. It concerns valuations and returns, not other outcomes in general. 'Outcomes' is broader than what the paper covers.
- *Limiti rispetto al nostro setting*: Studies returns (alpha, beta) of VC-backed companies, VentureXpert and VentureSource data 1987-2005, not exit probability; 1,934 companies in final sample; selection is on valuations/returns, not on exit outcomes.
- *Verbatim (solo per vedere dove finisce la fonte, MAI da copiare)*: "Valuations are observed only when a company has a refinancing or an exit event, and the endogeneity of these events is captured by the selection process."

### `puri2012`
- **Tesi (vincolante, l'unica cosa che puoi attribuirle)**: timing of failure over the life cycle.
- *Contesto dal lettore della routine (NON materiale: nulla che stia solo qui va nel testo)*: The txt shows that for VC-financed firms the failure risk is concentrated in a pattern over the life cycle: the lower cumulative failure rate of VC firms is driven by a much lower likelihood of failing in the first few years after receiving VC, and the difference then tapers off and loses significance. The tesi is a fragment about the timing of failure over the life cycle, which the paper does examine.
- *Limiti rispetto al nostro setting*: Compares VC vs non-VC firms from first VC receipt, not the gap between round 1 and round 2; US Census data 1981-2005.
- *Verbatim (solo per vedere dove finisce la fonte, MAI da copiare)*: "We find that the lower cumulative failure rate of VC-financed firms is driven by a much lower likelihood of failing in the first few years after initially receiving VC."
