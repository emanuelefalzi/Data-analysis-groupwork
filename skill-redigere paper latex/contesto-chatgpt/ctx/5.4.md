# Contesto — 5.4 Robustness

Parole obiettivo: **280** (±15%; nessun pavimento). Pagine di piano: 2.64.

**Ruolo**: show that the conclusions do not depend on the window, estimator, hype measure or omitted team quality.

## Convenzioni
- First person plural (we) is allowed for the design and our reading of the results; never first person singular.
- Present tense for theory and for what sources argue; past tense for the design and results of single studies; present tense for our own results as they appear in the tables.
- Associations only: never effect, impact, causes, leads to, reduces, increases for the gap or for hype. Write is associated with, goes with.
- Every number says what it is (logit coefficient, odds ratio, AME in percentage points, OLS coefficient), its p-value and its sample.
- Hypothesis verdicts use the exact wording of the paper (right sign, not significant; rejected, opposite sign; not supported).
- No verbatim quotes from sources: attributed paraphrase only.
- No bullet lists in the body: continuous prose. Tables and figures carry the numbers; the text points to them and does not repeat every cell.

## Verdicts and causal language (RULES.md)

- Hypothesis verdicts use the paper's Table 13 wording only: **right sign, not significant** · **rejected, opposite sign** · **not supported**. Never "no effect" or "confirmed".
- About the gap or hype, never write *effect, impact, causes, leads to, reduces, increases, drives*. Write **is associated with**, **goes with**.
- "Stable in sign" across hype measures, not "stable": with past-only hype β3 is significant (log L2621).


## Contenuto (l'unico materiale tuo, insieme ai numeri e alle fonti qui sotto)
1. **Window length**: *4/5/6-year windows, exit β1 never significant, failure β1 always significant, Figures 20–21*
   With four-, five- and six-year windows, exit β1 and β3 are never significant. Failure β1 is negative and significant in all three windows, and β3 is close to zero. The five-year choice drives none of the results (Figures 20–21).
2. **Estimator and no window**: *probit, β3 −0.028 (p = 0.390), exit ever, β1 −0.458, follow-up time*
   The probit gives the same signs and significance. Exit at any time gives a large negative gap coefficient, but it is not comparable: long-gap startups have less follow-up time left, so the estimate mixes gap with time.
3. **Past-only hype**: *β3 = −0.050 (p = 0.039), opposite to H2, 6,593 startups, 27 clusters*
   With hype computed from past years only, β3 becomes significant but stays negative: the gap penalty on exit is about 5% larger in odds per SD of past hype. It does not support H2 either. Smaller sample.
4. **Team and lead-age controls**: *founders, MBA, AI term, young lead < 6 years, β1 and β3 unchanged*
   Adding founders, an MBA founder, an AI term and a young lead investor leaves the gap coefficients unchanged, so omitted team quality of this kind does not explain the null. A young lead goes with lower exit odds.
5. **Further checks**: *finer hype, exit-market share, robust SEs, early exits removed, VIF < 2*
   Finer hype measures give β3 between −0.033 and −0.071, not significant. ⚠ The new log gives −0.036 to −0.078, with p = 0.090 for industry group × quarter: wording waits for the group (open point 21a). Robust SEs barely change p-values. Removing early one-round exits leaves β1 and β3 unchanged. VIFs are fine once the gap is centred.
6. **Hype as the raw VC share and a non-linear gap**: *raw share, β3 ≈ 0 in every window; hot − cold −0.9 pp (p = 0.650); squared gap not significant inside the windows; curved only with no window, where it mixes gap and follow-up time; Appendix B*
   Two checks added to the do-file on 20/11 (sections 12–13), with their tables and figures in Appendix B. First, hype as the **raw VC share** (the sector's percentage of all VC dollars in the year of round 1, not z-scored; the TA's suggestion) in place of `heat`. The gap × hype coefficient β3 is close to zero and not significant with 4-, 5- and 6-year windows and with no window, for exit and for failure (Tables B1–B2). At the mean ± 1 SD of the share (14.3% and 40.4%), the hot − cold difference in the AME of ln gap on exit is −0.9 pp (p = 0.650). With the share minus its sector mean, which keeps only the movement within each sector, β3 is −0.010 (p = 0.596). H2 is not supported with this measure either. Second, a **squared gap**, in ln(1 + months) and in months. Inside the 4-, 5- and 6-year windows no squared term is significant at 5% (at 10% only ln gap squared with the 6-year window: exit p = 0.070, failure p = 0.065; Tables B3–B4). In the 5-year models the linear and squared gap terms are not jointly significant (exit: χ²(2) = 0.32, p = 0.851 in ln and 1.85, p = 0.396 in months; failure: 2.42, p = 0.299 and 0.95, p = 0.622), but these tests are evaluated at a raw share of zero (see Guardrails). Only with no window does ln gap bend: the squared term is negative and significant for exit (−0.227) and for failure (−0.210), both p < 0.001, and the predicted probabilities of both fall at long gaps (Figures B1–B2). With no window part of this is time at risk: a longer gap leaves less time after round 2 to exit or to fail, the same lesson as point 2. The correlation heatmap (Figure B3) shows R and R × ln gap correlated at 0.94, in line with the high uncentred VIFs of point 5, while hype (`heat`) correlates at most 0.08 in absolute value with the group, the gap and the round-1 controls, and 0.31 with the raw share.

- **Tesi (vincolante, l'unica cosa che puoi attribuirle)**: grandstanding predicts young VCs bring firms to IPO earlier. That our young-lead coefficient goes the other way is our result (R-45), not the source's claim.
- *Contesto dal lettore della routine (NON materiale: nulla che stia solo qui va nel testo)*: The prediction in the txt is that young VC firms bring companies to IPO earlier, to build reputation. It is a prediction about IPO timing among IPO-backed firms, not about the probability of exit within five years. Whether our coefficient contradicts it is our result, not the source's.

## Trappole (guardrails della scaletta)
- **"Robust SEs give the same p-value, so the few clusters do not matter" is not a substitute for the wild bootstrap.** That limitation is in 5.6. Here state only the check.
- The past-only hype result is evidence about H2's sign. Its *interpretation* (signal extraction) is in 5.5.
- Do not call the exit-ever coefficient a result for H1. It is a lesson on why the window is needed.
- **Point 6, raw VC share.** The share is not centred (mean 27.4%, minimum 0.4%), so in these models β1 is the gap slope at a share of zero and the joint tests of the linear and squared gap terms are evaluated there too. Do not compare them with β1 of Tables 6 and 8, and do not write that the failure association "disappears" with the raw share or with a squared gap. β3 is per percentage point of share, not per SD: compare signs and p-values, not sizes.
- **Point 6, squared gap.** The models of do-file section 13 use the raw VC share as hype, not `heat`. The no-window curvature is not a result for H1: with no window part of it is time at risk (point 2). Do not describe Figures B1–B2 as an inverted U for exit in the windows: there the squared terms are not significant.
- **Point 6, floats.** Tables B1–B4 and Figures B1–B3 are in Appendix B, outside the 20 pages: the body keeps its 21 figures and 14 tables. Cite in the text only the two or three numbers that carry the point. The table row "R × ln(1 + months) × R × ln(1 + months)" is the squared term (do-file label).

**5.3 Valuation step (H3)**

[[TAB:T9]] reports OLS estimates for the valuation sample of 7,574 two-round startups. Standard errors are clustered by sector × year of round 1, allowing errors to be correlated among startups funded in the same sector and year. The OLS coefficient θ1 on the gap between the first and the second VC round is 0.109 (p < 0.001). H3 is rejected, opposite sign. This elasticity associates a 10% longer gap with a valuation step about 1% larger, and a move from 8 to 19 months across the interquartile range with a step about 8.9% larger.

The gap × hype OLS coefficient θ3 is −0.016 (p = 0.381), providing no evidence of moderation by hype. In [[FIG:F18]], the predicted log step rises from about 0.9–1.0 at very short gaps to about 1.2 at four years in both cold and hot markets, defined as hype at −1 and +1 standard deviation.

Larger first rounds have an OLS coefficient of −0.139. Older startups and more earlier deals are associated with steps 6.1% and 4.2% smaller, respectively (percentage changes are exp(coefficient) − 1 of the coefficients in [[TAB:T9]]), consistent with part of the growth being priced at round 1. Accelerator-led first rounds go with steps 33% larger. We discuss the pricing interpretation in [[SEC:5.5]] and the post-money and disclosure caveats in [[SEC:5.6]].

For 7,514 startups, [[FIG:F19]] shows a mean total valuation step approximately flat across the first four gap quintiles and higher, at 1.18 log points, in the longest-gap quintile. The median step per year falls from 1.89 to 0.36 log points: startups with short gaps gain value about five times faster per year. We read the positive θ1 as total progress accumulated before round 2 rather than speed of value creation, consistent with the competing prediction in [[SEC:2.4]]. Speed of value creation favours startups with short gaps.
