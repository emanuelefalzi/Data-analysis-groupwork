## Contenuto (l'unico materiale tuo, insieme ai numeri e alle fonti qui sotto)
1. **Fast rounds should come with larger steps**: *refinancing after value increases, upward selection, good news, step-up*
   Refinancing is more likely after value has risen, so observed round-to-round returns are selected upward. If fast rounds follow good news, they should carry larger valuation steps from round 1 to round 2.
2. **H3 stated**: *θ1 < 0, longer gap, smaller valuation step, two-round startups*
   H3: among two-round startups, a longer gap is associated with a smaller log valuation step, measured as the post-money valuation at round 2 over the post-money valuation at round 1 (θ1 < 0), as the good-news reading predicts.
3. **Progress takes time and is priced (our argument)**: *total progress, longer gap, larger total step, θ1 > 0, not from the literature*
   We argue that rounds are priced on what the venture has achieved. A longer gap leaves more time to achieve it, so the total step can rise with the gap even if value grows more slowly per month (θ1 > 0). This is our reading (I-03): the literature ties milestones to the release of funds within a round, not to the timing of the next one.
4. **Prices also reflect terms and bargaining**: *senior preferred, post-money overstates, terms ignored, reputation discount, bargaining power*
   Post-money valuations overstate fair value because later investors hold senior shares, and they ignore contract terms other than the equity share. Entrepreneurs accept lower prices from reputable VCs. Measured steps mix value with contract and bargaining terms.
5. **Hype enters the valuation model too**: *capital inflows, prices rise, moderation, θ3*
   Prices rise with capital inflows, so hype could move the step directly and change its slope in the gap. This is why the H3 model also includes hype and its interaction with the gap.

## Fonti citabili
### `cochrane2005` (citata insieme a korteweg2010)
- **Tesi (vincolante, l'unica cosa che puoi attribuirle)**: observed round-to-round returns are selected upward.
- *Contesto dal lettore della routine (NON materiale: nulla che stia solo qui va nel testo)*: Projects are more likely to get new financing when their value has risen, so mean returns of projects that get additional financing are an upward-biased estimate of the underlying mean return; volatility is biased downward.
- *Limiti rispetto al nostro setting*: Individual VC projects, VentureOne data to 2000 (dot-com era); returns, not exit probabilities; selection concerns valuations, not our exit outcome.
- *Verbatim (solo per vedere dove finisce la fonte, MAI da copiare)*: "the mean returns to projects that get additional ﬁnancing are an upward-biased estimate of the underlying mean return."

### `korteweg2010` (citata insieme a cochrane2005)
- **Tesi (vincolante, l'unica cosa che puoi attribuirle)**: observed round-to-round returns are selected upward.
- *Contesto dal lettore della routine (NON materiale: nulla che stia solo qui va nel testo)*: The paper says companies with higher valuations are more likely to be refinanced, so companies with higher shocks are overrepresented and the conditional mean of the shock in the observed data is positive. It also compares with Cochrane's (2005) round-to-round estimates, which do not correct for selection.
- *Limiti rispetto al nostro setting*: Studies returns (alpha, beta) of VC-backed companies, VentureXpert and VentureSource data 1987-2005, not exit probability; 1,934 companies in final sample; selection is on valuations/returns, not on exit outcomes.
- *Verbatim (solo per vedere dove finisce la fonte, MAI da copiare)*: "Intuitively, the problem arises whenever the probability of observing a valuation is related to the valuation itself."

### `kaplan2003`
- **Tesi (vincolante, l'unica cosa che puoi attribuirle)**: within a round, release of committed funds and some rights are contingent on milestones.
- *Contesto dal lettore della routine (NON materiale: nulla che stia solo qui va nel testo)*: The txt says that within a round (ex ante staging) part of the committed funding is contingent on financial or non-financial performance milestones. Between-round staging is analysed through the time until the next round, explained by repeat entrepreneur and industry variables, not by milestones. A new financing round is defined as differing from the contingent release of funds, because its price and terms are not set in advance.
- *Limiti rispetto al nostro setting*: Milestone contingency is within the round contract; our data observe whole rounds and the gap between them, not contract milestones.
- *Verbatim (solo per vedere dove finisce la fonte, MAI da copiare)*: "In an ex ante staged deal, part of the VC's committed funding is contingent on financial or non-financial performance milestones."

### `gornall2020`
- **Tesi (vincolante, l'unica cosa che puoi attribuirle)**: post-money overstates fair value by 48% on average for 135 US unicorns.
- *Contesto dal lettore della routine (NON materiale: nulla che stia solo qui va nel testo)*: The txt values 135 US unicorns with a contingent-claims model using contract terms from legal filings. Reported post-money valuations average 48% above fair value (median 37%), and overstate value in all cases, from 5% to 188%. The result is for unicorns only; 'about half' is a rounding of 48%.
- *Limiti rispetto al nostro setting*: Only 135 US unicorns (valuations above $1 billion) as of their most recent round, valued with a contingent-claims model on terms from charters; our sample is all US startups with a first VC round in 2012-2019 and mostly small post-money valuations.
- *Verbatim (solo per vedere dove finisce la fonte, MAI da copiare)*: "valuations average 48% above fair value, with 14 being more than 100% above."

### `ewens2022`
- **Tesi (vincolante, l'unica cosa che puoi attribuirle)**: post-money valuation is a poor measure of firm value because it ignores contract terms other than the equity share.
- *Contesto dal lettore della routine (NON materiale: nulla che stia solo qui va nel testo)*: The txt does not model or show investors trading valuation against contract terms. It shows (structural search-and-matching model) that higher-quality VCs offer more investor-friendly terms, which lower firm value, but the entrepreneur accepts them because VC quality raises startup value: the trade is terms versus VC quality. On valuation, it only says post-money valuation is a poor metric of firm value because it ignores terms other than the equity share, and proposes a term-adjusted valuation (about 15.7% lower in one example).
- *Limiti rispetto al nostro setting*: Seed/first-round contracts 2002-2015, structural model; no round-to-round valuation step.
- *Verbatim (solo per vedere dove finisce la fonte, MAI da copiare)*: "startup’s value increases with VC quality, and this value"

### `hsu2004`
- **Tesi (vincolante, l'unica cosa che puoi attribuirle)**: entrepreneurs accept lower valuations from more reputable VCs.
- *Contesto dal lettore della routine (NON materiale: nulla che stia solo qui va nel testo)*: Among competing offers made to the same start-up at its first professional round, offers from high-reputation VCs come with lower valuations: the VCs acquire start-up equity at a 10-14% discount. The author concludes that entrepreneurs are willing to accept a valuation discount to access the capital of better-reputed VCs. The evidence is a within-start-up comparison of offers, in a small sample of high-tech start-ups.
- *Limiti rispetto al nostro setting*: Small hand-collected sample of 51 MIT E-Lab high-tech start-ups, offers around 1995-2000, first professional round only; our data are all US PitchBook startups with a first VC round in 2012-2019. It concerns VC reputation, not the timing of rounds.
- *Verbatim (solo per vedere dove finisce la fonte, MAI da copiare)*: "entrepreneurs are willing to accept a discount on the valuation of their start-up in order to access the capital of VCs with better reputations"

### `heughebaert2012`
- **Tesi (vincolante, l'unica cosa che puoi attribuirle)**: university and government VCs value firms lower than independent VCs (Belgium).
- *Contesto dal lettore della routine (NON materiale: nulla che stia solo qui va nel testo)*: The paper hypothesises that VC firm types with more bargaining power vis-a-vis the entrepreneur negotiate lower valuations than types with less. In its Belgian sample, university and government VC firms (classed as higher bargaining power) value firms lower than independent VC firms, while captive VC firms do not differ. Bargaining power is attributed by VC type, not measured directly, and the lower valuations are read as consistent with the bargaining power argument. The result covers specific types, not investors with more bargaining power in general.
- *Limiti rispetto al nostro setting*: Belgian firms, first rounds 1988-2009, only 362 rounds; compares VC firm types (university, government, captive vs independent), not US PitchBook startups in 2012-2019; the outcome is premoney valuation, not exit.
- *Verbatim (solo per vedere dove finisce la fonte, MAI da copiare)*: "Consistent with this hypothesis, university and government VC firms, which have comparatively greater bargaining power, negotiate lower valuations compared with independent VC firms."

### `gompers2000`
- **Tesi (vincolante, l'unica cosa che puoi attribuirle)**: prices rise with fund inflows.
- *Contesto dal lettore della routine (NON materiale: nulla che stia solo qui va nel testo)*: Results show a strong positive relation between the valuation of venture investments and capital inflows; a doubling of inflows led to a 7% to 21% rise in valuation levels. The tesi as an association is supported.
- *Limiti rispetto al nostro setting*: Theory/data from 1987-1995 VentureOne valuations and fund inflows; our setting is 2012-2019 PitchBook and the outcome is exit within five years, not valuation.
- *Verbatim (solo per vedere dove finisce la fonte, MAI da copiare)*: "A doubling of in#ows into venture funds led to between a 7% and 21% increase in valuation levels."

## Sezione precedente (gia' scritta, per la continuita')
**2.3 Hot and cold markets**

Fund inflows were associated with higher private-deal valuations [[CIT-P:gompers2000]]. VC investment responded to industry Q [[CIT-P:gompers2008]]. IPO waves reflect changing market conditions [[CIT-P:pastor2005]]. Theory predicts that greater VC competition weakens investors' bargaining power and raises valuations [[CIT-P:inderst2004]]. In our setting, we view hype as a capital-supply condition, that is, as abundant capital.

Hot-market cohorts had more bankruptcies, but their IPO firms had higher valuations and more patents [[CIT-P:nanda2013]]. Investors in hot markets funded riskier ventures with less concern about follow-on financing risk [[CIT-P:nanda2013]]. These cohort differences are discussed in [[SEC:1]].

Early-stage VC deals fell sharply during recessions [[CIT-P:howell2020]]. In our setting, we argue that follow-on funding in cold markets depends partly on capital supply, not only on venture quality.

The gap between the first and the second VC round mixes market and venture components. If rounds close quickly for everyone in hot markets, a long gap is less informative about the venture. Our key hypothesis, H2, predicts a weaker negative association between the gap and exit within five years in hot markets: β3 > 0.

We propose signal extraction as a competing argument: in cold markets, most ventures wait, so a long gap says little about venture quality. We argue that, with easy money in hot markets, a long gap indicates investors chose not to fund quickly and is more informative about the venture. We cautiously predict β3 < 0 under this alternative.
