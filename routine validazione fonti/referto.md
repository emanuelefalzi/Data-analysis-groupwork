### 2.1 · Cornelli & Yosha (2003) (`cornelli2003`)
- **Tesi (scaletta):** staging with convertible securities keeps the option to stop.
- **What the source says:** The paper states that stage financing gives the venture capitalist the option to abandon the venture when the project's forward-looking value is negative. Its model shows that this option induces the entrepreneur to engage in window dressing, biasing the interim signal to reduce the probability of liquidation. Convertible securities are shown to deter this behaviour, because window dressing also raises the probability that the VC converts. The contribution is that convertibles dominate debt-plus-equity within staging, not that convertibles are what keeps the option to stop.
- **Quote (1. Introduction):** "The venture capitalist retains the option to abandon the venture whenever the forward looking net present value of the project is negative."
- **Proposed wording:** Cornelli and Yosha (2003) model stage financing as giving the venture capitalist the option to abandon the project, and show that convertible securities reduce the entrepreneur's incentive to window-dress interim performance to avoid liquidation.
- **Limits vs our setting:** Pure theory with no data; it concerns contract design (convertible debt or warrants) and not the timing of rounds, the gap between rounds or exit outcomes.
- **Note:** The tesi as worded attributes the option to stop to 'staging with convertible securities'. In the txt the option comes from staging itself (introduction); convertibles protect the quality of the signal on which the stop decision rests. The first part (staging gives the option to stop) is supported; the link to convertibles keeping the option is not worded this way.

- **What the source says:** In the model the posterior belief that the project is good falls in every period in which no success arrives (Bayes' rule). The model has only success or no success, not 'good news' as a graded signal. The decline is slower when beliefs are already near 0 or 1.
- **Quote (Section 2, The model (after equation 1)):** "The posterior belief at decreases over time if success doesn't arise."

- **Limits vs our setting:** Theory with no data; the signal is success vs no success, not startup rounds; the model does not talk about the time between funding rounds.
- **Note:** Tesi says 'without good news'; the txt speaks of 'no success' (success ends the project). Wording narrowed accordingly.
